Towing terms, made simple

The four numbers that matter most.

You do not need to be a truck expert. Start with these simple checks.

1

Trailer weight

What your trailer weighs after it is loaded with water, gear, tools, toys, animals, or camping equipment.

2

Tongue or pin weight

The downward weight the trailer puts on the truck. It counts against the truck’s payload.

3

Towing capacity

The maximum trailer weight the exact truck can pull under the matching RAM chart configuration.

4

Payload

Everything the truck carries: people, cargo, hitch equipment, accessories, and tongue or pin weight.

Why VIN lookup matters

Two similar trucks can have different answers.

These details can move a truck into a different RAM towing-chart row:

Engine

A RAM 1500 Pentastar, HEMI, Hurricane SO, and Hurricane HO are not one towing configuration.

Axle ratio

A small number on a door label can change the chart result. Use the VIN tool, then verify the physical truck if needed.

4x2 or 4x4

Do not use a 4x2 maximum to rate a 4x4 truck. The drivetrain changes weight and chart rows.

Cab and bed

More cab or bed can mean a different truck weight and a different towing number.

GVWR and rear wheels

Especially on heavy-duty trucks, GVWR and single versus dual rear wheels can affect tow and payload results.

Hitch type

A bumper-pull, fifth-wheel, and gooseneck have different equipment and weight considerations.
